Project Overview

The NEST (Non-stationary Emersed Structure for Takeoff-landing) is an autonomous surface vehicle (USV) specifically purpose-built to support Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) operations in maritime environments[cite: 2308]. Featuring a novel catamaran-style dual-hull configuration, it provides a highly stable 2.0 x 2.0 meter platform that enables UAVs to safely land during and after offshore inspection missions, such as monitoring floating photovoltaic (PV) plants[cite: 2314, 2316].

Technical Specifications

  • Dimensions: 2.0 m × 2.0 m × 1.3 m [cite: 2312]
  • Total Weight: ~100 kg [cite: 2312]
  • Available Payload: ~200 kg [cite: 2312]
  • Drive Mode: Vectorial drive (Holonomic navigation) [cite: 2312, 2336]
  • Max Speed: 1.5 m/s [cite: 2312]
  • Power Supply: LiFePO4 25.6 V @ 200 Ah (Operation time: ~2 h) [cite: 2312]
  • On-board Sensors: GPS, IMU, Compass, 3D LiDAR, Visual and Thermal cameras [cite: 2312, 2330, 2331]

Hardware & Software Architecture

The hardware architecture of the NEST USV follows a modular design, physically separating the power and electronic subsystems[cite: 2317]. The electronics module manages navigation, controlling the four thrusters, and integrates an onboard computer (NUC) with a CubeOrange flight controller[cite: 2323, 2324].

The software architecture is built around the ROS2 nav2 framework for autonomous navigation[cite: 2390]. The flight controller runs a modified version of the ArduSub firmware, which was adapted to support a vectored thruster configuration for holonomic position hold—a critical capability for safe UAV landing and takeoff procedures[cite: 2334, 2336, 2337]. The system employs a hybrid path planning approach, utilizing both global path generation and a local Adaptive Velocity Obstacle Avoidance (AVOA) algorithm[cite: 1969, 2439, 2441].
